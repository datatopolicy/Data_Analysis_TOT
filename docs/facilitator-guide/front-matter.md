# How to Use This Guide

This guide is the single reference for anyone facilitating the Data Analysis and Report Writing Training of Trainers (TOT). It is written for a lead facilitator and one co-facilitator working as a pair. Read it cover to cover at least two weeks before the course, then use the day tabs and session plans as your run-of-show during delivery.

## Conventions used in this guide

-**Session plans:**every session on the authoritative agenda has a full plan — time block, session title (verbatim from the agenda), 3–4 learning objectives, key content with the actual substance you will teach, a minute-by-minute activity plan, materials, facilitator notes, and a TRAINER TIP box on how participants will teach this back home.
-**Facilitator notes:**common pitfalls, pacing tips, and what to do when participants finish early or run late. Read the notes for the next session during every tea and lunch break.
-**TRAINER TIP boxes:**explain how the 24 participants — who will become in-country trainers — can adapt and re-teach each topic in Bangladesh, Côte d'Ivoire, Rwanda, and Tanzania.
-**Demo data references:**all examples use the fictional country "Demoland" and its 12 fictional districts. Patterns are described qualitatively (e.g., Westbrook's rising TB cases) — never quote exact numbers from memory; always say "as shown in the demo dataset" and read values live from the file so your numbers match the answer keys.

# Course Overview

## Background: the Data for Health Initiative

The Data for Health (D4H) Initiative, funded by Bloomberg Philanthropies and implemented with the CDC Foundation, partners with governments to strengthen the systems that generate, analyze, and use public-health data — with a particular emphasis on civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) and routine health information systems such as DHIS2. Better data on births, deaths, and causes of death is the foundation for better policy: governments cannot reduce what they cannot count.

Within D4H, the Data Analysis Project builds national capacity to turn raw health data into accurate, equity-focused, decision-ready reports. Experience across D4H countries shows that data collection has often outpaced data use — registries and DHIS2 instances hold years of data that are rarely cleaned, rarely disaggregated, and rarely written up. This project closes that gap by training government analysts to clean data in Excel, compute standard indicators, visualize results honestly, and write reports that policymakers actually read.

## Goal of the Data Analysis Project

*To institutionalize sustainable, equity-focused data analysis and report-writing capacity inside ministries of health and national statistics offices, so that every country can routinely produce high-quality health reports from its own CRVS and routine data systems.*

## The eight project objectives

1. Strengthen national capacity in descriptive epidemiology using tools already available in government offices, principally Microsoft Excel.
1. Improve the completeness, quality, and documented use of CRVS data, including cause-of-death information.
1. Increase the routine analysis and use of DHIS2 and other routine health information system data for program monitoring.
1. Embed health-equity analysis — disaggregation by sex, age, geography, and other PROGRESS-Plus dimensions — as standard practice in every report.
1. Institutionalize standard operating procedures (SOPs), data dictionaries, and report templates for data access, cleaning, analysis, and writing.
1. Build a cadre of certified national trainers able to cascade data-analysis training to subnational staff.
1. Improve translation of analytic findings into clear, actionable recommendations for policymakers and program managers.
1. Establish monitoring and evaluation of data-use practices so progress can be tracked and course-corrected.
## The seven project phases

The Data Analysis Project runs in seven phases. This TOT course is Phase 1 — everything else builds on the trainers it produces.

|**Phase**|**Name**|**What happens**|
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Training of Trainers (this course) | 24 mid-level government professionals (6 each from Bangladesh, Côte d'Ivoire, Rwanda, and Tanzania) master descriptive analysis in Excel, equity-focused reporting, and adult training methods. |
| 2 | Country adaptation & cascade training | National teams adapt the curriculum, translate materials where needed, and train subnational analysts in-country. |
| 3 | Supervised country data-analysis projects | Country teams analyze their own national datasets with remote mentoring from the project technical team. |
| 4 | Report production & technical review | Teams draft full IMRAD-structured reports; drafts pass through structured technical review before release. |
| 5 | Dissemination & policy dialogue | Findings are presented to ministry leadership and program managers; recommendations feed into planning cycles. |
| 6 | Institutionalization | SOPs, data dictionaries, and report templates are formally adopted into ministry procedures and job descriptions. |
| 7 | Evaluation & sustainability planning | Outcomes are evaluated against the M&E framework; countries plan how the capacity is maintained after project funding ends. |

## Participant profile

24 participants, organized as 4 country teams of 6 (Bangladesh, Côte d'Ivoire, Rwanda, Tanzania). They are mid-level government health professionals — typically holding a master's degree with 3+ years of experience — who already work with health data. Baseline skills: Excel fundamentals (SUM, AVERAGE, sort/filter); familiarity with CRVS and DHIS2 concepts. After this TOT they will become the in-country trainers for Phase 2 cascade trainings, so every session should be delivered with one eye on content mastery and the other on how they will re-teach it.

# Course Learning Objectives

By the end of the five days, participants will be able to:

1. Explain the Data for Health Initiative, the Data Analysis Project's goal, objectives and phases, and their own role as national trainers in Phase 2 cascade trainings.
1. Apply a health-equity lens to data analysis using the PROGRESS-Plus framework, identifying which dimensions of inequality their country's data can and cannot illuminate.
1. Set precise, answerable data-analysis objectives that link a policy or program question to the right data, indicators, and disaggregations.
1. Clean real-world-style messy datasets in Excel (Text-to-Columns, Remove Duplicates, TRIM, find-and-replace, validation) and combine datasets with VLOOKUP/XLOOKUP so analysis starts from trustworthy data.
1. Calculate core descriptive indicators in Excel — counts, proportions, incidence and notification rates using census denominators, means, medians, and measures of spread — and choose the right summary for the data at hand.
1. Use pivot tables to produce stratified analyses (by sex, age group, district, year) efficiently and reproducibly.
1. Structure a technical report using IMRAD (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion), and draft policy-ready recommendations.
1. Design honest, publication-quality tables, figures, and district maps in Excel following best-practice rules for clarity and accessibility.
1. Produce and present a 10-slide country-team mini analysis report from synthetic data, and defend its methods and conclusions under questioning.
1. Plan and deliver a cascade training session back home using adult-learning training methodologies (demonstration, guided practice, feedback).
# Monitoring & Evaluation Framework

## Pre/post-test plan

A written knowledge test is administered twice: Monday 9:00 during welcome (pre-test, 30 minutes) and Friday 16:00 during wrap-up (post-test, 30 minutes). The two forms are parallel — same blueprint, different item wording — so score gains reflect learning, not memorization.

-**Format:**25 multiple-choice questions (1 point each) + 2 short-answer questions (5 points each) = 35 points total.
-**Blueprint:**5 items on D4H/project concepts and equity; 5 on data cleaning and combining datasets; 5 on rates, descriptive statistics, and pivot tables; 5 on IMRAD, tables, figures, maps, and recommendations; 5 on training methodologies. Short answers: (1) compute and interpret an incidence rate from a small table; (2) critique a flawed chart and rewrite its takeaway.
-**Pass standard:**post-test score of 70% (24.5/35) plus demonstrated competence in the group project. The test measures knowledge; the group project measures applied skill.
-**Logistics:**print 30 copies of each form; participants write a 4-digit anonymous code (last 4 digits of phone number) on both forms so pre/post can be matched without names. Co-facilitator scores within one week; aggregate results feed the course report.
-**Use of results:**report mean gain and % reaching the pass standard overall and by country team. Items missed by >50% of participants flag topics needing reinforcement in Phase 2 cascade trainings.
## Daily evaluation forms

A one-page form is distributed at 16:45 each day and collected before participants leave. It asks: (1) the most useful thing learned today (free text); (2) one thing still unclear (free text); (3) pace rating (too slow / about right / too fast); (4) confidence to teach today's hardest topic back home (1–5 scale). The co-facilitator tallies responses each evening; the lead facilitator addresses the top 2–3 unclear items in the next morning's debrief. This is the course's early-warning system — use it.

# Group Project Guidance

## The assignment

Working in their 4 country teams of 6, participants produce a 10-slide mini analysis report using the Demoland synthetic datasets, presented on Friday morning (Day 5, 9:30–11:30). Each team chooses one analytic focus from the menu below — first-come, first-served at the end of Day 1, so all four topics are covered across teams:

1. TB trends and equity, 2021–2025: national trend, Westbrook's rise, sex and age patterns, district comparison.
1. TB case notification rates using census denominators: which districts carry the highest burden once population size is accounted for?
1. Mortality registration completeness: quantify and visualize Riverside's under-registration and discuss implications for interpreting mortality indicators.
1. Subnational disparities in TB burden: rank districts, map the burden, and identify priority districts for intensified case finding.
Each 10-slide report must contain: title slide; analysis objectives (1 slide); methods and data sources including cleaning steps (1–2 slides); results with at least 2 publication-quality tables and 2 figures (4–5 slides); equity interpretation (1 slide); recommendations (1 slide). Presentations are 15 minutes + 5 minutes of questions.

## Assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–4. 4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning, 0 = missing. Maximum 40 points; 28+ = proficient.

|**#**|**Criterion (weight ×1 each)**|**What 'proficient (3)' looks like**|
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Analysis objectives | Objectives are specific, answerable, and linked to indicators and disaggregations. |
| 2 | Data cleaning & methods transparency | Cleaning steps documented; messy-data issues in Demoland files identified and handled. |
| 3 | Correct indicators and rates | Numerators/denominators correct; rates use census population; multipliers stated. |
| 4 | Equity disaggregation | Results stratified by at least sex and district; PROGRESS-Plus gaps honestly noted. |
| 5 | Table quality | Follows best-practice rules: clear titles, units, no gridline clutter, aligned decimals. |
| 6 | Figure quality | Honest axes (zero baseline for bars), readable labels, color-blind-safe palette, takeaway title. |
| 7 | IMRAD structure | Report follows Introduction–Methods–Results–Discussion; no results in methods, no new data in discussion. |
| 8 | Recommendations | Specific, actionable, audience-appropriate; linked to findings; no overclaiming beyond the data. |
| 9 | Presentation delivery | Within 15 minutes; all members speak; slides support rather than duplicate speech. |
| 10 | Response to questions | Answers accurately; admits limits of the data when appropriate. |

> **TRAINER TIP**— When teams present, require one slide titled 'What we cannot say from this data' — it trains the humility that separates trustworthy analysts from dangerous ones, and it previews the limitations section of IMRAD. Back home, make this slide mandatory in every cascade-training project.
