## Day 5 (Fri 26 Mar)  |  9:30–10:30

### Group report presentations

### Learning objectives

1. Deliver a 15-minute team analysis presentation within time.
1. Defend methods and conclusions under questioning.
1. Give specific, kind, rubric-tied feedback to peer teams.
### Key content and talking points

Presentations 1 and 2 (15 minutes each + 5 minutes questions). Facilitators score silently against the 10-criterion rubric during each talk; the lead keeps time with a visible timer and stops overtime firmly but kindly ('Thank you — let's hold questions for what we heard').

Feedback protocol after each presentation (during the 5-minute question window + 2-minute facilitator close): one strength named specifically, one improvement tied to the rubric, then audience questions. Model the 'specific, prompt, kind' feedback standard from Tuesday — participants will copy it at home.

What to listen for as an assessor: correct rate math, honest axes, the equity slide, the 'what we cannot say' humility, and recommendations that pass the four tests.

### Activity plan (minute-by-minute)

|**Clock**|**Min**|**What happens**|
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9:30–9:50 | 20 | Team 1: 15 min presentation + 5 min questions. |
| 9:50–9:52 | 2 | Transition. |
| 9:52–10:12 | 20 | Team 2: 15 min presentation + 5 min questions. |
| 10:12–10:30 | 18 | Facilitator feedback closes (strength + rubric-tied improvement per team); buffer for overruns. |

### Materials needed

- Projector + timer
- Rubric scoring sheets (facilitators)
- Submitted slide decks
### Facilitator notes — pitfalls, pacing, contingencies

-**Stop overtime kindly but firmly —**overruns steal the next team's time and teach that the clock is negotiable. It is not.
-**Score during the talk,**not after — memory of detail fades within minutes.
-**Protect the question window:**if the audience is shy, the lead asks the first question to break the ice.
> **TRAINER TIP**— Run cascade-training final presentations with the same 15+5 discipline and the same feedback protocol. Trainees who have been assessed fairly assess fairly — the culture replicates.

### Unstructured team working time (no formal session)

The source agenda schedules no formal session in this block. It is protected, unstructured team working time — for finalizing presentation slides, incorporating morning feedback into decks, rehearsing, and informal facilitator consultations. Do not program it: the value is the absence of programming.

- Facilitators remain available at tables for questions; no plenary, no new content.
- Teams that presented in the morning use the time to note feedback for their Phase 2 cascade decks.
- Co-facilitator finalizes rubric tallies and prepares score summaries.
> **TRAINER TIP**— Unstructured time is a feature, not a gap — defend it against the urge to 'fill' it. In cascade trainings, protect at least one such block: it is where struggling trainees catch up without stigma.

## Day 5 (Fri 26 Mar)  |  14:00–15:00

### Training Methodologies

### Learning objectives

1. Draft a 1-page Phase 2 cascade-training plan (audience, objectives, adaptation, materials, risks).
1. Present and defend the plan under peer questioning.
1. State the trainer's creed and its practical meaning.
### Key content and talking points

The second methodologies session turns participants from practitioners into planners. Each country team drafts a 1-page cascade-training plan for Phase 2 using the session-design template from Tuesday:

1. Audience: who exactly (cadre, numbers, baseline skills), where, when (proposed dates within 6 months).
1. Objectives: 3–4 learning objectives for a 3-day in-country course, adapted from this week's list.
1. Content adaptation: which sessions to keep, shorten, or replace; what local data replaces Demoland; language needs (French materials for Côte d'Ivoire).
1. Materials: what exists (this guide, .docx template, checklists) vs. what must be built or translated.
1. Risks and mitigations: the top 3 things that could derail the cascade (e.g., laptop shortages, approval delays) and the plan for each.
Teams present their 1-page plan in 5 minutes each (14:40–15:00); plenary pressure-tests one risk per team. Facilitators collect the plans — they are the Phase 2 baseline documents.

Close with the trainer's creed: 'Teach the skill, not the slides. Demo, deconstruct, practice, feedback. Protect team time. End on time.'

### Activity plan (minute-by-minute)

|**Clock**|**Min**|**What happens**|
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14:00–14:10 | 10 | Lead frames: from practitioner to planner; the 5-part 1-page plan. |
| 14:10–14:40 | 30 | Country teams draft the plan (use Tuesday's template + adaptation flipcharts from Thursday). |
| 14:40–15:00 | 20 | 4 × 5 min plan pitches + risk pressure-test. Collect plans. |

### Materials needed

- Session-design template
- Thursday adaptation flipcharts (photos)
- 1-page plan worksheet
### Facilitator notes — pitfalls, pacing, contingencies

-**Keep pitches to 5 minutes —**the evaluation session must start at 15:00.
-**Push on risks:**vague risks ('funding') get the follow-up 'and what specifically will you do in April about it?'
-**These plans are deliverables —**collect every copy; they anchor Phase 2 follow-up calls.
> **TRAINER TIP**— The 1-page cascade plan is the single most important take-home document: a plan that fits on one page gets executed; a 20-page plan gets filed. Require the same 1-page discipline in every training you commission.


## Day 5 (Fri 26 Mar)  |  15:00–16:00

### Preparing for country-specific data analysis projects

### Learning objectives

1. Inventory national datasets, owners, and access routes.
1. Draft candidate analysis questions with five-part objectives.
1. Complete the 1-page Country Project Charter.
### Key content and talking points

Bridge from synthetic Demoland to real national data. For each country team, work through the readiness checklist:

1. Data inventory: which datasets exist (CRVS, DHIS2, census/survey), who owns each, what the access route and timeline is (recall Day 1's SOP session).
1. Question shortlist: 2–3 candidate analysis questions per country, each with a draft five-part objective.
1. Denominator check: is a usable census or projection available at the needed disaggregation? If not, that is the first work package.
1. Equity check: which PROGRESS-Plus dimensions can the national data actually support? Name the gaps now.
1. Team and timeline: named analysts, a 90-day milestone sketch (data access → cleaning → analysis → draft report → review).
1. Support needed: what mentoring the project technical team should provide, and when.
Teams capture this on the Country Project Charter (1 page, in workbook); each team reads its question shortlist aloud (5 min each, 14:50–15:10 within this hour's flow below). Facilitators reality-check one objective per team against the five-part anatomy.

End with the handoff: 'Demoland was practice. Your country's data is the performance. The project team will contact your focal point within two weeks to schedule the first mentoring call.'

### Activity plan (minute-by-minute)

|**Clock**|**Min**|**What happens**|
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15:00–15:15 | 15 | Lead: the readiness checklist; Demoland → real data handoff. |
| 15:15–15:45 | 30 | Teams complete the Country Project Charter. |
| 15:45–16:00 | 15 | Plenary: question shortlists read aloud (3–4 min each); facilitator reality-checks one objective per team. |

### Materials needed

- Country Project Charter worksheet
- Day 1 SOP flipchart photos
- Five-part objective anatomy (wall reference)
### Facilitator notes — pitfalls, pacing, contingencies

-**Guard against over-ambition:**a charter with six questions will deliver none — force the shortlist to 2–3.
-**Name the focal point for mentoring contact**before the session ends; 'someone will follow up' evaporates.
-**Collect the charters —**like the cascade plans, they are Phase 2/3 baseline documents.
> **TRAINER TIP**— The Country Project Charter ritual — inventory, questions, denominators, equity, team, timeline, support — is reusable for any technical-assistance kickoff. Run it at the start of every country engagement, not just trainings.


## Day 5 (Fri 26 Mar)  |  16:00–17:00

### Workshop evaluation and wrap-up

### Learning objectives

1. Complete the post-test under standard conditions.
1. Evaluate the course honestly across all dimensions.
1. State personal next steps as a national trainer.
### Key content and talking points

Close the week with the same rigor it opened with:

1. Post-test (16:00–16:30): same conditions as Monday — 30 minutes, same anonymous 4-digit code, parallel form. Collect at 16:30 sharp.
1. Course evaluation (16:30–16:45): the overall evaluation form (objectives met? most/least useful session? facilitator feedback? venue? confidence as a future trainer, 1–5). Collect before certificates — response rates collapse otherwise.
1. Results preview (16:45–16:55): lead shares the week's arc — pre-test mean (from Monday), project highlights, the 'Our vision' flipchart revisited: 'Which of these did we move toward this week?' Promise the full M&E summary (pre/post gains, rubric scores) by email within two weeks.
1. Certificates and close (16:55–17:00): certificates to all 24 participants; group photo; travel briefing for Saturday 27 March; final words — 'You arrived as analysts. You leave as trainers.'
Facilitator back-office (after 17:00): score post-tests within one week; finalize rubric tallies; write the course report within three weeks (agenda, attendance, pre/post analysis, rubric summary, daily evaluation themes, cascade plans and charters annexed, recommendations for Phase 2).

### Activity plan (minute-by-minute)

|**Clock**|**Min**|**What happens**|
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16:00–16:30 | 30 | Post-test in silence; collect at 16:30. |
| 16:30–16:45 | 15 | Overall course evaluation form; collect before certificates. |
| 16:45–16:55 | 10 | Week's arc: vision flipchart revisited; M&E summary promised. |

### Materials needed

- Post-test forms (30)
- Course evaluation forms (30)
- Certificates (24)
- 'Our vision' flipchart from Day 1
### Facilitator notes — pitfalls, pacing, contingencies

-**Collect the evaluation BEFORE certificates —**say it plainly: 'Forms first, then certificates.' It works.
-**Keep the closing words under 60 seconds —**the week should speak for itself; brevity is the final modeling of the clock discipline you preached.
-**Do not promise post-test scores on the spot;**promise the summary by email within two weeks, and deliver it.
> **TRAINER TIP**— The evaluation-before-certificates sequence is a portable trick for any training: it routinely doubles response rates. And always close by revisiting the vision set on day 1 — it turns a course into a story with an ending, which is what trainers remember when they teach.
