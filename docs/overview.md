# **1. Background**

The Data for Health (D4H) Initiative, supported by Bloomberg Philanthropies and implemented with the CDC Foundation, partners with governments to strengthen the collection and use of public health data for policy and program decision-making. Across participating countries, ministries of health collect large volumes of routine surveillance and program data, yet too often these data are under-analyzed and under-used: reports are delayed, findings are not disaggregated to reveal inequities, and recommendations are too vague to act on.

This five-day Training of Trainers (TOT) course addresses that gap. It builds a cadre of national trainers — six per country team from Bangladesh, Côte d'Ivoire, Rwanda, and Tanzania (24 participants total) — who can both produce high-quality analyses and reports themselves and teach these skills to others through in-country cascade trainings. The course uses a single running synthetic dataset (tuberculosis notifications for the fictional country "Demoland," 2021–2025) so that every concept is practiced on realistic, policy-relevant material.

# **2. Course Goal**

**Goal:** To build a cadre of national trainers able to design and deliver high-quality data analysis and report-writing training in their home countries, so that routine health data are analyzed rigorously, communicated clearly, and used for equitable decision-making.

# **3. Learning Objectives**

By the end of the course, participants will be able to:

1. Formulate clear, policy-relevant analysis objectives and answerable analysis questions.
1. Assess data quality (completeness, timeliness, validity, consistency) and document limitations transparently.
1. Clean, validate, and combine datasets using reproducible spreadsheet workflows.
1. Calculate and interpret descriptive statistics and rates using appropriate population denominators.
1. Design accurate, honest tables, charts, and maps that follow data-visualization best practices.
1. Write a structured IMRAD report with specific, actionable, audience-targeted recommendations.
1. Apply adult-learning principles and facilitation techniques to train others effectively.
1. Develop a country action plan for cascade training, mentored projects, and institutionalization of templates and SOPs.
# **4. Seven Phases of the TOT Program**

- **Phase 1 — Pre-TOT preparation (Jan–Mar 2027).** Country teams formed; pre-work assigned (data inventory, draft analysis objectives); logistics confirmed.
- **Phase 2 — In-person TOT workshop (22–26 Mar 2027, Kigali).** This course: intensive skills building, group analysis project, facilitation practice.
- **Phase 3 — In-country cascade trainings (Apr–Sep 2027).** Each country team delivers a 4-day training to 10–15 national/subnational staff.
- **Phase 4 — Mentored country analysis projects (Apr 2027–Mar 2028).** Each country team completes one full analysis-and-report cycle on a national priority topic with remote mentoring.
- **Phase 5 — Virtual technical support and peer learning (ongoing).** Monthly check-ins, peer review of draft reports, shared resource library.
- **Phase 6 — Institutionalization (Jul 2027–Mar 2028).** Report templates, data dictionaries, and SOPs formally adopted into ministry routines.
- **Phase 7 — Final evaluation and sustainability planning (Mar 2028).** Endline assessment, lessons-learned workshop, and handover to national training institutions.
# **5. Participant Profile**

- 24 government health professionals: 6 per country team (Bangladesh, Côte d'Ivoire, Rwanda, Tanzania).
- Roles: monitoring & evaluation officers, surveillance officers, health statisticians, program managers, and health information system focal points.
- Prerequisites: routine work with health data; basic Excel proficiency; commitment to deliver an in-country cascade training within 6 months.
- Working language: English (with French-language support materials available for Côte d'Ivoire participants).
